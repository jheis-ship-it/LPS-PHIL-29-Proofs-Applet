Rules Reference

Basic Rules

&E (Simplification): p & q ⊢ p (and ⊢ q)
&I (Conjunction): p, q ⊢ p & q
-->E (Modus Ponens): p --> q, p ⊢ q
-->I (Cond. Proof): Hypothesis p ... derive q ⊢ p --> q
vI (Addition): p ⊢ p v q
vE (Constructive Dilemma): p v q, p --> r, q --> r ⊢ r
~I (Indirect Proof): Hypothesis p ... derive q & ~q ⊢ ~p
~E (Double Neg. Elimination): ~~p ⊢ p
<-->I: p --> q, q --> p ⊢ p <--> q
<-->E: p <--> q ⊢ p --> q (or q --> p)

Derived Rules

MT: p --> q, ~q ⊢ ~p
HS: p --> q, q --> r ⊢ p --> r
DS: p v q, ~p ⊢ q (or p v q, ~q ⊢ p)

Equivalence Rules

DM: ~(p & q) :: ~p v ~q  |  ~(p v q) :: ~p & ~q
DN: p :: ~~p
MI: p --> q :: ~p v q
TRANS: p --> q :: ~q --> ~p
EXP: (p & q) --> r :: p --> (q --> r)

Equivalence rules may be applied to the whole line or to any subformula within it. Every compound subformula is displayed with its own parentheses unless it is the whole (terminal) formula.

Proof Strategies

  • Look at the conclusion's main connective first. A conditional (p --> q) usually calls for -->I: take p as a hypothesis, derive q. A negated conclusion (~p) usually calls for ~I: take p as a hypothesis, derive a contradiction.
  • Break down conjunctive assumptions immediately with &E.
  • If you have p --> q and ~q, reach for MT.
  • Chain conditionals with HS instead of two separate -->E's.
  • Rewrite awkward assumptions with equivalence rules — MI, TRANS, DM can reshape a formula into something usable.
  • For theorems (no assumptions), you must generate your own material via a Hypothesis.
  • Discharge as soon as you can once inside a subproof.
